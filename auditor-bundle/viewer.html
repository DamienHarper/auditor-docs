Audit Viewer

Web interface for browsing audit logs

The bundle includes a web interface for browsing audit logs.

✨ Enabling the Viewer​

# config/packages/dh_auditor.yaml
dh_auditor:
    providers:
        doctrine:
            viewer: true

Or with options:

dh_auditor:
    providers:
        doctrine:
            viewer:
                enabled: true
                page_size: 50

🛤️ Routes​

The viewer registers these routes:

RouteURLDescription
dh_auditor_export/audit/exportExport of audit entries (streamed)
dh_auditor_list_audits/auditList all audited entities
dh_auditor_show_entity_stream/audit/{entity}/{id?}Entity audit stream
dh_auditor_show_transaction_stream/audit/transaction/{hash}Transaction details
dh_auditor_show_user_stream/audit/user/{id}Activity timeline of one user
dh_auditor_select_revisions/audit/{entity}/{id}/revisionsPick two revisions of an object
dh_auditor_compare_revisions/audit/{entity}/{id}/compareCompare two revisions of an object

Route Configuration​

With Symfony Flex, routes are auto-configured. Otherwise, create config/routes/dh_auditor.yaml:

dh_auditor:
    resource: "@DHAuditorBundle/Controller/"
    type: auditor

The viewer routes come from this import only, so its prefix applies to all of them. With prefix: /admin, the viewer answers at /admin/audit:

dh_auditor:
    resource: "@DHAuditorBundle/Controller/"
    type: auditor
    prefix: /admin

IMPORTANT

The routing.controllers resource of the symfony/routing recipe (config/routes.yaml) does not load the viewer routes: an application that imports only routing.controllers has no viewer. Import the bundle routes as above.

📄 Pages​

The viewer tells each action (a database transaction) as a phrase, such as alice changed Status: Draft → Published and added to Tags: symfony and audit, with its technical details one click away. The phrases come from the history phrases: translate or describe them to speak the words of your application. The objects they name link to their history, and are marked (deleted) when they no longer exist (when their identifier is an integer or a string; an object hidden by a Doctrine SQL filter, such as a soft delete, is marked as well).

Overview (/audit)​

  • Recent activity: the last 10 actions across the audited entities the reader may view, with what they were done on (alice on Post “Why audit?”: …). An action touching many objects is summed up by type (deleted 38 Post); it is read whole, whatever the number of its entries (a page reads 1,000 entries at most: past that, the largest actions tell only their most recent entries).
  • What is audited: the audited entities (their name from the dh_auditor_fields domain, key FQCN), their number of actions (as their stream counts them, cached with the activity graph when its cache is on and no read_scope is set), their latest activity and, when enabled, their activity graph. The list can be sorted by name, number of actions or latest activity (?sort=name_asc, name_desc, count_desc, count_asc, activity_desc, activity_asc).

Object History (/audit/{entity}/{id})​

The history of one object and of the entities attached to it (#[AuditRoot], roots entity option, see Histories), newest first, grouped by day:

  • one row per action: icon, author (linked to their actions), phrase, time, origin (Web, Command line, Messenger, System), blame context and, when the author was impersonated (switch user), who impersonated them; the whole row leads to the action page, the objects it names to their own history; its details list every change with its raw field and values (long texts compared word by word; a text shows between quotes only when it would be misread without them: empty, with spaces at its ends, or looking like a number, a boolean or null), the transaction and the blame, and link to the action page;
  • filters: who, what (insert, update, remove, associate, dissociate), field (needs native JSON search), origin (http, console, messenger, system), when (last 7, 30 or 90 days), and, when entities can be attached, with or without them;
  • Show older actions reads the next page_size actions through a cursor (?after=): a link, which viewer.js loads in place; the header counts the actions of the history;
  • Who changed what last: the 8 fields changed most recently, with who changed them and when (or at creation);
  • Technical side: class, identifier, audit tables read, number of entries.
/audit/App-Entity-Post/42?who=12&what=update&field=status
/audit/App-Entity-Post/42?origin=console&when=30&attached=0

Only the view role of the object's class is checked: its history shows the entries of the attached entities even to a reader who cannot view their class (their links to their own history are hidden).

Entity Stream (/audit/{entity})​

Every action on an entity, with the filters of the stream: type, user (__anonymous__ for the entries without user), changed field, and period.

NOTE

The changed field filter requires native JSON support (MySQL 5.7+, MariaDB 10.2.3+, PostgreSQL 9.4+, SQLite 3.38+) and an audit table migrated to the schema v2 structure (audit:schema:migrate). It is hidden otherwise. On large audit tables, combine it with a period: the database has to read the diffs of every entry left by the other filters.

/audit/App-Entity-User?type=update&user=42
/audit/App-Entity-User?type=insert&user=__anonymous__
/audit/App-Entity-User?field=email
/audit/App-Entity-User?date_from=2026-01-01&date_to=2026-03-31

The dates are days, inclusive, in the dh_auditor.timezone time zone. Filters are kept by the pager.

Pages hold page_size actions (transactions), whatever their number of entries: every page but the last holds as many actions, each one whole. A page reads 1,000 entries at most: past that, the small actions stay whole and the largest ones share the rest, each telling only its most recent entries. The header counts the actions the filters keep, and a page beyond the last one shows the last one. The actions of a page are found by grouping the entries the filters keep by transaction in the database: on a large audit table, a filter (type, user, period) keeps that cheap.

Action (/audit/transaction/{hash})​

One action: its phrase, the list of what it did, then every entry it wrote (up to 200), with its table and id, raw fields and values, the transaction (and the date of its ULID), the blame and the raw diffs, which can be copied as JSON. Opened from the history of an object (?root=App\Entity\Post:42), it links to the previous and next actions of that history.

Actions of a User (/audit/user/{id})​

Everything a user did, across the audited entities the reader may view, newest first, each action with what it was done on (On Post “Why audit?”: …), with filters on the entity (on), the type (what) and the period (when). The user is identified by the blame_id recorded with each entry (up to 255 characters).

  • Anonymous entries (no blame_id) have no page. Neither do entries recorded with an empty blame_id, which is what happens when the Symfony user class has no getId() method.
  • Pages hold page_size actions, as in the entity stream: an action that wrote in several audit tables is one action, shown and counted once (the audit tables of one connection are read in one statement).

Compare Revisions (/audit/{entity}/{id}/compare)​

From the history of an object, Compare two states lists its revisions: pick two of them to see the state of the object before and after the changes made between them, field by field. The page links to the previous and next revisions, so that the comparison can be moved along the history.

📤 Export​

/audit/export streams audit entries, so that large exports do not load everything in memory:

ParameterDescription
formatndjson (default, recommended for large exports), json or csv
entityEntity to export (App-Entity-User); all the entities the user can view when omitted
idObject ID
from, toCreation date range (ISO 8601, e.g. 2026-01-01)
blame_idUser ID
anonymize1 to remove the user (ID, name, class, firewall, impersonator) and the IP address
/audit/export?entity=App-Entity-User&format=csv&from=2026-01-01

Each entry carries the blame as flat fields: blame_id, blame_user, blame_user_fqdn, blame_user_firewall, blame_impersonator_id, blame_impersonator and blame_impersonator_fqdn (the user who impersonated the author, if any), ip and blame_via.

The export follows the same access rules as the viewer (see Access Control): an entity the user cannot view is refused, or left out when no entity is given. From the command line, the provider's audit:export command offers the same filters.

📊 Activity Graph​

Each entity card displays a visual sparkline graph showing audit activity over time: one bar per day, the number of actions (database transactions) of that day, as the stream of the entity counts them on that day. A bar leads to that stream.

Configuration​

dh_auditor:
    providers:
        doctrine:
            viewer:
                enabled: true
                activity_graph:
                    enabled: true       # Show/hide the graph (default: true)
                    days: 30            # Number of days to display (default: 30, max: 30)
                    layout: 'bottom'    # Graph layout: 'bottom' or 'inline' (default: 'bottom')
                    cache:
                        enabled: true   # Enable caching (default: true)
                        pool: 'cache.app'  # Cache pool service ID
                        ttl: 300        # Cache TTL in seconds (default: 300)

Layout Options​

LayoutDescription
bottomSparkline displayed below the entity info, full width (default)
inlineCompact sparkline displayed in the header row, next to the number of actions

Caching​

The activity graph uses caching to improve performance on large audit tables. The number of actions shown for each entity on the audit index page is counted as the entity stream counts them, and cached with the graph (same lifetime and tag): a count may lag behind by up to the cache lifetime, as the graph does. Without the activity graph, or with its cache disabled, the actions are counted on every page load.

With a read_scope, the graph and the number of actions count only the actions the scope lets the reader see, and are never cached: the scope depends on the reader (a tenant, for instance), so they are computed on every page load.

NOTE

Caching requires symfony/cache:

composer require symfony/cache

If symfony/cache is not installed, the graph will still work but without caching.

Cache with Tags​

If your cache pool supports tags (TagAwareCacheInterface), the bundle uses the tag dh_auditor.activity for efficient cache invalidation.

🗑️ Clear Cache Command​

Clear the activity graph cache manually:

# Clear all activity graph cache
php bin/console audit:cache:clear

# Clear cache for a specific entity
php bin/console audit:cache:clear --entity="App\Entity\User"

IMPORTANT

Clearing all cache requires a cache pool that supports tags. If your cache pool doesn't support tags, you can only clear cache for specific entities.

Display Behavior​

StateBehavior
Graph disabled (enabled: false)Activity graph section is completely hidden
Graph enabled, no dataPlaceholder with "No recent activity" message
Graph enabled, with dataBars are normalized (tallest bar = 100%)

🎨 Theme​

The viewer follows the conventions of shadcn/ui without depending on it: every color of its templates is a CSS variable, exposed to Tailwind as a dh-* color (dh:bg-dh-card, dh:text-dh-muted-foreground, dh:border-dh-input…). The palette is neutral and the primary button near black; the teal of the auditor logo marks the activity bars, the selected revision and the focus ring (dh-brand), and, darkened to read as text, the links, the time of an action and the current page (dh-link). The icon of an action tells its meaning: green for a creation, red for a deletion, blue for any other change. Controls share one size (dh:h-8, dh:rounded-lg), badges are pills (dh:h-5).

Every Tailwind class of the templates carries the dh: prefix (dh:hidden, dh:sm:inline, dh:hover:bg-dh-accent…), and the theme variables of Tailwind in bundles/dhauditor/app.css are --dh-* ones (--dh-spacing, --dh-text-sm…): none of them meets a class or a variable of an application that uses Tailwind too, whatever the order of the stylesheets on the page.

The tokens live in src/Resources/assets/css/theme.css, already compiled into bundles/dhauditor/app.css:

TokenRole
--dh-background, --dh-foregroundPage
--dh-card, --dh-card-foregroundCards, tables
--dh-primary, --dh-primary-foregroundPrimary button (Apply), checkboxes
--dh-muted, --dh-muted-foregroundTable headers, icons, secondary text
--dh-accent, --dh-accent-foregroundHovered and selected rows
--dh-border, --dh-input, --dh-ringBorders, control borders, focus ring
--dh-brand, --dh-brand-from, --dh-brand-toTeal of the logo (bars, selection), its gradient
--dh-deleted, --dh-inserted (+ -foreground)Removed and added text in the diffs
--dh-warning (+ -foreground, -border)Notices of the comparison page
--dh-link, --dh-link-foregroundLinks, the clickable time of an action, current page
--dh-create, --dh-update, --dh-delete (+ -foreground)Icon of an action by its meaning

Redefine any of them to restyle the viewer, after the bundle stylesheet (for instance in a stylesheet block of an overridden layout.html.twig):

:root { --dh-primary: #4f46e5; --dh-brand: #4f46e5; }
.dark { --dh-primary: #a5b4fc; --dh-brand: #a5b4fc; }

🌙 Dark Mode​

The viewer includes a dark/light mode toggle button in the header. The dark values of the tokens apply under a dark class on an ancestor, set on <html> by viewer.js.

Behavior​

  • Default: Follows system preference (prefers-color-scheme)
  • Manual toggle: Click the sun/moon icon in the header
  • Persistence: User preference is saved in localStorage

The toggle works without page reload and persists across sessions.

🔐 Content Security Policy​

The viewer carries no inline script, event handler attribute nor style attribute: its behaviours (theme, sort of the entity list, filters submitted on change, technical mode, copy of the raw diffs, older actions loaded in place) live in bundles/dhauditor/viewer.js, loaded by layout.html.twig, and its styles in bundles/dhauditor/app.css and bundles/dhauditor/preflight.css. It therefore works under a strict policy such as script-src 'self'; style-src 'self', as long as the bundle assets are served from your origin (bin/console assets:install).

🔒 Access Control​

Via Configuration​

dh_auditor:
    providers:
        doctrine:
            entities:
                App\Entity\User:
                    roles:
                        view:
                            - ROLE_ADMIN

Via Attributes​

#[Audit\Auditable]
#[Audit\Security(view: ['ROLE_ADMIN'])]
class User {}

Via Symfony Security​

# config/packages/security.yaml
security:
    access_control:
        - { path: ^/audit, roles: ROLE_ADMIN }

The rule must match the path the viewer is imported at: with prefix: /admin, write ^/admin/audit.

WARNING

When neither the entity nor its scope requires roles, the default role checker grants the viewer to every visitor. Protect the imported path with an access_control rule (or a firewall) unless the audits are meant to be public.

NOTE

In association diffs (associate / dissociate), the related entity links to its audit stream only when the current user can open it: the entity must be audited and its view roles granted. Otherwise it is displayed as plain text. Templates can apply the same rule with the dh_auditor_is_viewable(entity) Twig function (an entity object or class name).

🔗 URL Format​

Entity namespaces use dashes instead of backslashes:

App\Entity\User → App-Entity-User

Helper methods:

use DH\AuditorBundle\Helper\UrlHelper;

// Namespace to URL parameter
$param = UrlHelper::namespaceToParam('App\Entity\User');
// Returns: 'App-Entity-User'

// URL parameter to namespace
$namespace = UrlHelper::paramToNamespace('App-Entity-User');
// Returns: 'App\Entity\User'

🔧 Generating URLs​

Link your own pages to the viewer with the AuditViewerUrlGenerator service (autowirable) or its Twig functions. They hide the route names and parameters of the viewer, and the encoding of the entity class in its URLs.

Twig​

{# Audit stream of an entity: an object, or a class name with an optional id #}
<a href="{{ dh_auditor_entity_url(post) }}">History</a>
<a href="{{ dh_auditor_entity_url('App\\Entity\\Post') }}">All posts</a>
<a href="{{ dh_auditor_entity_url('App\\Entity\\Post', 42, {what: 'update'}) }}">Updates of post #42</a>

{# Transaction and user timeline #}
<a href="{{ dh_auditor_transaction_url(entry.transactionId) }}">Transaction</a>
{# ... opened from the history of post #42: the action page offers its previous and next actions #}
<a href="{{ dh_auditor_transaction_url(entry.transactionId, root: 'App\\Entity\\Post:42') }}">Transaction</a>
<a href="{{ dh_auditor_user_url(entry.userId) }}">User activity</a>

{# Absolute URL, e.g. in an email #}
{{ dh_auditor_entity_url(post, absolute: true) }}

Each function returns null when the viewer cannot show the page: viewer disabled, auditor disabled, entity not audited, entity not persisted yet, or an entry without user (anonymous change) or without transaction id. Access rights are not checked: combine the link with dh_auditor_is_viewable() to show it only to users allowed to open it.

{% set history_url = dh_auditor_entity_url(post) %}
{% if history_url and dh_auditor_is_viewable(post) %}
    <a href="{{ history_url }}">History</a>
{% endif %}

PHP​

use DH\AuditorBundle\Viewer\AuditViewerUrlGenerator;
use Symfony\Component\Routing\Generator\UrlGeneratorInterface;

public function __construct(private AuditViewerUrlGenerator $auditUrls) {}

// Audit stream of an entity object (its identifier is read from the entity)
$url = $this->auditUrls->generateEntityUrl($post);

// Audit stream of a class, of one object, with viewer filters
$url = $this->auditUrls->generateEntityUrl(Post::class);
$url = $this->auditUrls->generateEntityUrl(Post::class, 42, ['what' => 'update', 'who' => '42']);

// Absolute URL
$url = $this->auditUrls->generateEntityUrl($post, referenceType: UrlGeneratorInterface::ABSOLUTE_URL);

// Transaction and user timeline
$url = $this->auditUrls->generateTransactionUrl($entry->transactionId);
$url = $this->auditUrls->generateUserUrl($entry->userId);

The filters are the query parameters of the page: type, user, field, date_from, date_to and page for the stream of an entity; what, who, field, origin, when and attached for the history of an object, which gets the type and user filters as what and who. The identifier of an entity object is converted like the object_id stored in the audit table (UUID, enum, custom type), and a Doctrine reference is not loaded to read it.

🎨 Customization​

Override Templates​

Create files in templates/bundles/DHAuditorBundle/:

templates/bundles/DHAuditorBundle/
├── Audit/
│   ├── audits.html.twig              # Overview
│   ├── object_history.html.twig      # History of an object
│   ├── entity_stream.html.twig       # Stream of an entity
│   ├── transaction_stream.html.twig  # Action
│   ├── user_stream.html.twig         # Actions of a user
│   ├── revisions.html.twig           # Revisions of an object
│   ├── compare.html.twig             # Comparison of two revisions
│   └── helpers/
│       ├── helper.html.twig          # Helper macros
│       └── pager.html.twig           # Pagination
├── History/
│   ├── _actions.html.twig            # Actions grouped by day, and the link to older ones
│   ├── _row.html.twig                # One action
│   ├── _details.html.twig            # Technical details of an action
│   ├── _macros.html.twig             # Icons, origin badges, day headings, change rendering
│   ├── phrase.html.twig              # A phrase
│   └── object_reference.html.twig    # An object named by a phrase
├── Component/
│   └── History.html.twig             # <twig:DhAuditor:History> component
└── layout.html.twig                  # Base layout

NOTE

The changeset of a JSON column is a deep diff: a tree whose leaves are {new}, {old} or {new, old}. A JSON object whose own keys are new/old therefore looks like a field diff, so a custom template must not tell them apart by their keys. Use entry.isDeepDiff(field) for the root of a changeset, and the dh_auditor_is_diff_leaf(node) Twig function for the nodes inside a deep diff.

IMPORTANT

bundles/dhauditor/app.css only contains the classes of the bundle templates, with the dh: prefix. An overridden template styles itself with prefixed classes and the dh-* tokens (dh:bg-dh-card, see Theme); a class without the prefix, or that no bundle template uses, such as the 7.x bg-slate-50 or text-indigo-600, has no style. An overridden page of Audit/ keeps the <div data-dh-auditor-viewer> element that wraps the content of its dh_auditor_content block: the reset of the viewer applies under it.

Custom Layout​

{# templates/bundles/DHAuditorBundle/layout.html.twig #}
{% extends 'base.html.twig' %}

{% block stylesheets %}
    {{ parent() }}
    {# the viewer, with its own reset, bounded to its content: the rest of your page is left as it is #}
    <link rel="stylesheet" href="{{ asset('bundles/dhauditor/app.css') }}">
    {# behaviours of the viewer (theme, sort, filters, technical mode, copy, older actions); loaded without defer so
       that the theme applies before the first paint #}
    <script src="{{ asset('bundles/dhauditor/viewer.js') }}"></script>
{% endblock %}

{% block body %}
    <div class="container">
        {% block dh_auditor_content %}{% endblock %}
    </div>
{% endblock %}

bundles/dhauditor/app.css holds the dh: classes of the viewer, its tokens and its controls. It may come before or after the stylesheets of your application, which keeps its own classes, theme and font: the sans-serif text of the viewer is the one of your page, only its code and a few signs use the stacks of the --dh-font-mono and --dh-font-sans variables (:root { --dh-font-sans: var(--font-sans); } to change the latter). It does not reset your page either: its reset, the one of Tailwind (Preflight) cut down to what the templates need, only reaches the content of the pages of the viewer, under a data-dh-auditor-viewer element, so that the viewer looks the same with or without a reset of your own. The layout of the bundle adds bundles/dhauditor/preflight.css, the whole Preflight of Tailwind; a layout that extends yours does not need it.

WARNING

The rules of the viewer sit in the cascade layers of Tailwind (base, components, utilities). A rule of your application outside any layer (Bootstrap, plain CSS such as select { padding: … }) wins over all of them on the elements it matches, whatever the order of the stylesheets. Put such rules in a layer, or scope them out of the viewer. An application that compiles the viewer itself (see Styling) does not load app.css as well.
